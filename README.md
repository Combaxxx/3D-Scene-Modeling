1. How do I approach designing software?

I approach designing software by first determining what the final product needs to accomplish and then breaking the project into smaller pieces that can be developed individually. During this course, I improved my ability to plan a visual scene and determine how simple shapes could be combined to create more complex objects. For my final 3D scene, I had to think about the size, position, rotation, color, texture, and material of each object while also considering how everything would look together in the completed scene.

My design process involved starting with the basic structure of the scene and gradually adding more detail. I created objects from simple 3D shapes before moving on to textures, materials, camera movement, and lighting. This helped keep the project manageable because I could focus on one part at a time. I can apply this same process to future projects by breaking larger problems into smaller components and making sure each component works before adding additional complexity.

2. How do I approach developing programs?

My approach to developing programs became much more iterative throughout this project. Instead of expecting everything to work correctly the first time, I learned to make smaller changes, run the program, observe the results, and then make adjustments. This was especially important when working with object transformations, textures, lighting, and camera controls because even small changes in the code could have a noticeable effect on the scene.

Iteration was a major part of developing my 3D scene. Many objects went through several versions before I was satisfied with their appearance. I adjusted dimensions, positions, textures, materials, and lighting throughout the milestones. I also encountered problems where changes to one part of the scene affected another part, which required troubleshooting and testing to determine the cause. By the end of the course, I was much more comfortable modifying existing code and understanding how different parts of the program worked together. This approach will be useful in future software projects because testing and improving code throughout development can make complex projects easier to manage.

3. How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics and visualization gave me experience with concepts that I had not worked with extensively before this course. I learned how 3D objects can be constructed from simpler shapes and how transformations, textures, materials, lighting, and camera positioning work together to create a complete visual scene. I also gained more experience working with C++ and OpenGL and learned how mathematical concepts can directly influence what is displayed on the screen.

These skills can help me in my future educational pathway because they strengthened my understanding of C++, problem solving, debugging, and working with larger projects containing multiple files and systems. Professionally, I may not work specifically with OpenGL, but many of the skills I developed are transferable to other areas of software development. Learning how to troubleshoot complex code, work with interconnected components, and continuously improve a program through testing and iteration will be useful regardless of the specific language, framework, or technology I work with in the future.
